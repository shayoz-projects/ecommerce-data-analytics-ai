## Recommendations Summary

Consolidated from the AI insight layer (V2/V3 outputs). Each item is a proposed test, not a guaranteed outcome — consistent with the project's AI Requirements (no unsupported claims presented as fact).

## 1. Investigate the box-to-individual purchasing shift
Finding: Bundle (box) share of revenue declined from 38.6% (2023) to 33.7% (2025) — a real, multi-year trend, not a single bad year.
Proposed action: Review historical Wix catalog data to check whether box listings were reduced, changed, or under-promoted over this period, and whether this shift reflects customer preference or supply-side changes.

## 2. Build the "Exclusive Royal Blanket" into a mini product line
Finding: This single product has the top revenue index (100.0) — more than double the next-highest item — across 337 units and 320 orders.
Proposed action: Since this product already anchors both revenue *and* the top cross-sell pair (see #3), test bundling it with its most common pairing at a slight discount, rather than just monitoring it passively — we already know what pairs with it, so use that.

## 3. Formalize the top cross-sell pairing
Finding: "Baby bear towel" and "Exclusive Royal blanket" were bought together in 79 orders — the strongest co-purchase signal in the data (raw count only; lift/confidence not calculated).
Proposed action: Test featuring these two items together on the product page or checkout flow, and measure whether joint purchase rate changes.

## 4. Build on improving retention
Finding: Repeat customer rate rose steadily and consistently-3.7% (2023) → 6.1% (2024) → 10.9% (2025) — real, validated improvement, even as new-customer orders declined over the same period.
Proposed action: Test a post-purchase communication step (reminder, loyalty offer) aimed at repeat customers, since retention is already trending positively and may respond well to reinforcement.

## 5. The "DIY gift bundle" behavior behind individual purchases is fading
Finding: Orders with no box at all ("pure individual" purchases) used to average 3.43 items per order (2022) — customers essentially building their own gift bundle piece by piece. That's fallen steadily to 2.19 items by 2026, a steeper decline than box orders show over the same period.
Interpretation: The earlier finding that individual items are gaining revenue share doesn't necessarily mean customers are replacing boxes with equally full "DIY bundles״ - that behavior itself is weakening. Individual purchases are becoming smaller and more casual over time, not a full substitute for the box experience.
Proposed action: Test whether prompting individual-item buyers with "complete your gift" suggestions at checkout (using the cross-sell pairs already identified) recovers some of this lost per-order item count, rather than assuming customers will keep self-bundling on their own.

---
Data limitations that apply across all five items: no product cost/margin data (profitability can't be assessed), no discount or traffic data, and box/individual classification is inferred from naming patterns rather than confirmed catalog status. These caveats are carried over from the underlying AI outputs, not omitted here.
